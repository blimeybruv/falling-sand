# Element ideas

A menu of materials for contributors to pick from, grouped into chains so each one leads to
another. Recipes should follow from the physics where possible, so players can guess them in
Discovery mode. See `CLAUDE.md` for how to add a material and make it discoverable.

**Effort:** **T** = a table entry plus a `Reaction`, no code · **F** = one small function in
`behaviours.ts` · **S** = needs a new system.

**Status:** ✅ built · blank = up for grabs.

## Vents (generators, the scientific way)

Real "spouts" are reservoirs under pressure finding a way out, so generators are **vents**: a static
cell that releases a material into an open neighbour. Each vent builds up pressure (its `life`
counter refills over time) and releases in bursts when it's full, and only into open air, so no vent
can flood the grid like an endless tap. Proposed engine change: one new table field, something like
`emits: { what, rate, direction, burst }`, so each vent is mostly data. Geyser rock is half of one
already.

## Clouds, redesigned (21-22 Nov)

**Why clouds had lost their body.** Measured on a 7,950-cell cloud drawn as three thick strokes:
1,830 cells were left after 10 s and none after 20 s. With rain turned off, about 7,940 were left
after 10 s and 10 after 20 s. Two things were eating it:

- **Pouring.** Any cloud deeper than a few rows rained at a rate that grew with its depth. That
  emptied the cloud in seconds, from the bottom up.
- **Lifetime.** Every cloud cell had a lifetime of 10-20 s, so whatever the rain left still
  vanished.

Cells also rose one at a time, so a cloud streaked into strands, and the lone-wisp rule then ate
the strands. A person expects a cloud to hang there as a white heap with a darker base, rain only
when it's heavy, and stay after the rain.

**The design: three kinds of cloud, each with a shape people know.**

- **Cumulus (Cloud).** Persistent: it has no lifetime and no fade, and only evaporates as a lone
  wisp or in sunshine.
  - Below the cloud layer, every column rises a row on the same frame, every 20 frames, so the cloud
    floats up as one block.
  - At the layer it puffs up into domes, and loose cells settle into gaps beside the body.
  - It drizzles only now and then.
- **Storm cloud.** Where a cloud is piled 20 or more rows deep (14 until 22 Nov), its base darkens into storm cloud.
  - Storm cloud pours, harder the deeper the cloud above it.
  - It charges and strikes lightning, the only cloud that does. Strikes come one at a time, at
    least 40 frames apart, however big the storm.
  - Once fewer than 8 rows of cloud are left above it, it clears back to cumulus. A storm rains
    itself out into a fair-weather cloud of real depth, not into nothing. The gap between 14 and 8
    stops cloud flickering between the two kinds.
- **Cirrus.** Water vapour that climbs well above the cloud layer becomes thin, wind-blown streaks.
  Cirrus never rains and fades over about a minute. It's what the water cycle leaves high in the
  sky.

**Later (22 Nov, afternoon).** Lone wisps now evaporate back into invisible vapour instead of
vanishing. Vapour reaching the cloud layer hangs there until it touches cloud or enough of it
has gathered, then condenses. Clouds keep their water, and condense in clumps rather than as lone
specks. Cirrus stops climbing about 14 rows above the cloud layer, so it streams across the sky
rather than piling into a white band along the top.

**Fluffier (22 Nov, evening).** Clouds still looked like wavy ribbons hanging from the cloud
layer (droopy, with straight slopes), and strong sun burnt them off in seconds.

- **A level base.** Every cloud now sits on a level base just below the cloud layer: columns rise
  or sink onto it as whole columns.
- **Heaping.** Each frame, a cloud's target is a half-ellipse 3.5 times as wide as it is tall,
  holding the same amount of cloud, with broad lumps. Cloud flows from the thin edges towards the
  middle until it gets there, so a cloud gathers into a cumulus heap: flat underneath, domed on top.
- **Rounding.** A surface-tension rule melts spikes and corners into the dents beside them.
- **Bigger heaps tower.** Storm cloud now needs 20 rows of cloud above it, so ordinary heaps stay
  fair-weather, and it clears below 14.
- **Slower burn-off.** The sun burns cloud off five times more slowly, so clouds keep their body
  through a sunny minute.
- **Gases.** Clouds now move through other gases (lightning's ozone used to jam them).
- **Shading.** Each cell's depth is blended with its neighbours' above, so light spreads softly
  down from every bump and the cloud reads as rounded lobes.

**Rendering.** Each cloud material gets its own depth-shading table, a white top darkening
downwards, so a storm base reads as a different colour, not just as a deep cloud.

**Gotcha.** Gases fade over their last `fadeFrames` frames of life, and a gas with no lifetime
has life 0. A persistent gas needs `fadeFrames: 0`, or it renders invisible. That fix also
applies to Neon.

## Anomalies (design note, 21 Nov)

Suggested by the user: rare cells where one law of physics goes wrong, in the spirit of anomaly
zones in fiction, but our own designs (names, looks and rules), in the game's pseudo-scientific
style. Each is a single static cell with a bounded reach (a radius of a few cells, scanned a few
cells a frame, so it costs little), breaks exactly one rule, and leaves the rest of the physics to
react to it. They should be rare and found rather than given: made by extreme events (lightning
striking a magnet inside plasma, a red sprite touching the ground, a diamond hit by a blast), and
perhaps a late Discovery tab of their own. Each could have a sound (a hum, a hiss, a low throb).
A larger feature: best built after the cloud redesign.

**Built 26 Nov (the first five).** They have their own palette tab, Anomalies. Each is indestructible
by blasts and acid (it can still be erased), and a low throb sounds while any is on the grid.
Differences from the plan:
- **Cold sun** freezes water into thin ice, which thaws again once it's out of reach. Ordinary ice
  keeps itself cold and spreads, so a cold sun by the sea would slowly have frozen the whole sea.
- **Lift field**: lightning striking a magnet beside plasma (lightning through hydrogen makes the
  plasma).
- **Gravity knot**: it scans every cell within reach each frame, not a few, because a sampled pull
  lost to ordinary gravity. Grains caught by it stop falling the ordinary way.
- **Stasis pocket**: cells within reach update one frame in eight, staggered so they move in slow
  motion rather than in jerks.

**The last three (26 Nov, late).**
- **Phase fault** shifts things within reach one state up through their `above` change, leaving them
  just past its temperature, so they change back as they cool a little way off.
- **Linked gates**: every gate is linked to every other, rather than in coloured pairs (single cells
  drawn with the brush would almost never share a colour). They register with the world each frame,
  like stasis pockets, at most 64. Made by lightning striking an arc node.
- **Transmuter**: a second Discovery reward, earned by finding the other seven anomalies. Chains: rust
  to iron to copper to gold, coal or charcoal to graphite to diamond, sand or stone to quartz. It
  burns out into slag after 30 to 60 seconds.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ✅ | Lift field | Gravity | Loose particles nearby fall upwards, so sand and water fountain into the sky and rain down outside it | Lightning striking a magnet in plasma | F |
| ✅ | Gravity knot | Gravity | Pulls loose particles in from all sides and crushes them: sand into stone, coal into diamond | A diamond caught in a blast | F |
| ✅ | Cold sun | Heat flows downhill | Draws heat out of everything around it, so it freezes water and puts out fire, and glows brighter the more it drinks | Dry ice touching plasma | F |

| ✅ | Arc node | Insulation | Throws arcs of electricity to anything nearby, conductor or not, setting things alight | Lightning striking graphite under salt water | F |

style, not borrowed from any particular mythos. The usual rules hold: single cells only (a creeping

## Figures (design note, 22 Nov): only if the rule changes

`CLAUDE.md` allows materials and single-cell creatures only: no people, stick figures or
multi-cell entities. **Don't build this unless the user changes that rule** (and writes the
exception into `CLAUDE.md`). Recorded so the idea isn't lost: the user suggested that if entities
ever come, tiny minimalist figures (a few flat-coloured pixels, in the spirit of minimalist
pixel-art games) would fit best. Our own figures and behaviours, not a recreation of any particular
game, its fighting, characters or look.

- **Separate from the grid**: a figure is a small sprite drawn over the world, with its own
  position and velocity, not cells in the simulation, so "cells are materials" stays true and the
  renderer's one pass over the grid stays as it is (figures are drawn on top afterwards).
- **The fun is the physics they land in**: they walk on solids, climb small steps, fall, swim or
  sink in liquids, slow down in mud and quicksand, get pushed by wind and thrown by explosions,
  catch fire, get struck by lightning (a rod nearby protects them), slip on ice, shelter from rain.
- **Simple and bounded**: a cap on how many exist, a few behaviours (wander, climb, flee fire, seek
  shelter, follow light at night), no combat system, nothing that needs pathfinding over the whole
  grid.
- **Opt-in**: a setting or its own palette tab, so the sandbox stays a materials game by default.
- **Scenes**: figures would need their own section in the scene format (bump `VERSION`, keep old
  scenes loading without them).

through lighter liquids. Missing, easiest first:

**Built 27 Nov.** Impacts: glass and stained glass (force 250, into sand), thin ice (100, into
water), icicles (150, into snow); fruit splats after 14 frames of falling. Blocks of ice aren't
brittle: hail (force up to 480) would smash the ice that makes clouds hail. Structural collapse:
stone and basalt span 10, concrete and obsidian 8, brick 6, ice 5, glass 3, thin ice 1, pinned
powders 2; metal, wood and everything else never collapse (so trees stand, and an iron girder
bridges any gap). Loose powder supports what rests on it but doesn't hold a beam from the side.
Not saved in scenes (like the Edges setting).

**Built 27 Nov, morning.**
- **Weight on top** (with collapse on): one pass down each column adds up the densities resting on
  each structural cell, back to the last gap of air. A structural cell with open space beneath it
  gives way when that passes its `strength.load`: glass 250 (about 12 rows of sand), ice 400, brick
  1200, concrete 1600, obsidian 1500, stone and basalt 2000, thin ice 30, pinned powders 60.
  Structure passes load down without adding its own, and anything resting on something solid passes
  it straight on, so only floors and bridges over a gap can be crushed.
- **Connected vessels** (always on), the first half of liquid pressure: every third frame, each body

  resting surface stands 3 or more rows above its lowest moves a cell from the top of the high side

  and flowing water are left alone. Not yet: fountains (water spurting above its level) and dams
  bursting, which need real pressure. About 0.1-0.2 ms a frame on a grid half full of water.

## Load-bearing materials (design note)

Built 27 Nov as structural collapse (see "Physics depth" above). The original note:

Feasible in its simple form; a session's work.

- **Model:** each structural material gets a `strength`: how many cells it can reach from solid
  support (say stone 12, wood 6, brick 4, glass 2). A new per-cell `support` value is that cell's
  distance from support (the grid floor, or a column standing on it), recalculated with one
  relaxation pass per frame, the same kind of cost as temperature.
- **Failure:** past its strength, a cell gives way into its loose form (stone and brick into
  gravel, wood into a new sawdust or splinters powder) and falls. Because support moves outward a
  cell per frame, collapses ripple across a bridge rather than vanishing at once, and blasting out a
  column brings down what it held up.
- **Not worth it:** real weight and stress (crushing, tension in beams): an iterative solver, far
  costlier and fiddlier for little visible gain.
- **The catch:** most existing scenes have floating stone shelves. Make it a toggle ("Structural
  collapse", off by default to begin with), and record the setting in the scene format (bump
  `VERSION`, keep old scenes loading with it off).

## Notes

- Electricity (lightning, conducting metal, sparks) is the biggest missing system: a per-cell
  charge that travels through conductors. Worth designing on its own before building on it.
- Avoid reactions that fire constantly in ordinary sandbox play; slow ones (chances of 0.001–0.01
  per frame) feel like nature, fast ones feel like a bug.
